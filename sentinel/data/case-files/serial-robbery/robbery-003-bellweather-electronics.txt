INCIDENT REPORT

At 06:50 AM on 6 March 2025, operations manager Mr Leon Farr arrived at
Bellweather Electronics Ltd to open the premises and found the secondary
loading-bay door had been breached. The door's deadbolt had been defeated
using an instrument consistent with a slim-jim or bypass tool. No alarm
activation had been transmitted to the monitoring station overnight.

Officers attended at 07:08 AM. The premises houses a distributor and retail
outlet for professional audio, imaging, and computing equipment. The warehouse
section adjoins the retail showroom via an internal connecting door, which
was also found open on arrival.

---

STOLEN PROPERTY

The following items are confirmed missing:

  - Professional digital imaging equipment (cameras, lenses, accessories):
    estimated value £18,700
  - High-specification laptop and tablet computers (22 units):
    estimated value £21,400
  - Portable audio interfaces and studio-grade headphones (11 units):
    estimated value £5,200

Total estimated loss: £45,300

Items were removed selectively from a warehouse section. Non-portable and
bulky equipment was left in place. Officers note the selection was efficient
and suggests prior knowledge of the stock layout.

---

FORENSIC FINDINGS

Fingerprint examination of the loading-bay door and internal connecting door
returned two partial latent prints. Both were of insufficient quality for
database comparison.

Tyre impression evidence: officers recovered a partial tyre impression on a
muddy grass verge at the rear of the premises adjacent to the loading bay.
Examination identified the impression as consistent with a commercial light
goods vehicle, estimated tyre width 185–195 mm. A dark blue Ford Transit was
observed on residential CCTV approximately 400 metres from the premises at
02:11 AM on 6 March 2025. The registration plate was not captured clearly
on the available footage.

Alarm system investigation: Bellweather Electronics Ltd uses a Safeguard
Pro Series alarm system — a different vendor from SentryGuard. The Safeguard
Pro Series panel was placed into a test and bypass mode at 23:38 on 5 March
2025. A Northstar Facilities Ltd visit record confirms an engineer attended
the site on 2 March 2025, three days before the burglary, to conduct a
routine alarm inspection.

Northstar Facilities Ltd paperwork for the 2 March visit identifies the
attending engineer as D. MERCER. This is the first time an engineer name
appears in Northstar Facilities documentation recovered in connection with
Operation Nightfall.

---

INVESTIGATION NOTES

The tyre impression and vehicle sighting are consistent with a dark blue
Ford Transit. No registration plate has been obtained from the footage
captured near Bellweather Electronics Ltd.

Detective Whitmore has reviewed Case MCR-2025-0101 and Case MCR-2025-0148
and notes that the series methodology is consistent across all three incidents:
a Northstar Facilities Ltd engineer visit shortly before burglary, an
authorised credential used to place the alarm into a non-operational mode,
selective and efficient removal of high-value portable stock, and no alarm
activation transmitted.

Bellweather Electronics Ltd uses a different alarm vendor (Safeguard Pro
Series) from the first two incidents (SentryGuard). The series method has
been adapted to operate across alarm vendor boundaries, suggesting an
individual with broad technical knowledge of commercial alarm systems.

The name D. MERCER on the Northstar Facilities work order has been referred
to the Major Crimes Unit. Enquiries are proceeding to identify and locate
this individual. Northstar Facilities Ltd has been requested to provide
D. MERCER's employment record and credential access history.

---

CONCLUSION

Investigation active. Third confirmed incident in Operation Nightfall series.
Priority line of enquiry: identity and movements of D. MERCER.
